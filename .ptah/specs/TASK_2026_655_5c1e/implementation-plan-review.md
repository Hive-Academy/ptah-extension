The loop is bounded: the request fires in the `finally` after the releases (so at most one resync is in flight per tab), another overflow needs 2000 new live chunks inside that resync's own span, and the reload rate is the live event rate divided by 2000 — churn at worst, never a storm, and it ends when the stream ends. R-F7 (both variants) and R-B8 pin the mechanism end to end, including that the fresh seed's merged delta contains the early-delivered text in arrival order (the projection observed them in arrival order). The transient between a post-reset overflow and the fresh resync (reversed/duplicated render) is explicitly documented by the plan (plan lines 644-647) and repaired by the fresh resync, which R-F7's variant asserts.
2. **Minor — D-5 recency was implicit: RESOLVED.** Component 7 "Resync recency" (plan lines 573-590): `myGeneration = ++resyncGeneration` and `latestResync.set(loadKey, myGeneration)` are taken synchronously on arrival before any `await` (arrival order is snapshot order because `handleStreamSnapshot` → `resyncStream` → the `switchSession` prefix are synchronous, `chat-message-handler.service.ts:530-536`), the rule is checked after every pre-claim `await` (each wait-loop wake-up) and immediately before claiming, the `finally` deletes the entry owner-checked, and a woken waiter with an older generation returns without registering or claiming. ## Proportionality

The two verified causes are: (1) hidden-tail overflow → `resyncRequired` → the resync wipes the in-flight `message_start` and turn status; (2) `publishTerminal`/`reveal` delete per-tab state → the per-tab sequence restarts → the frontend drops later snapshots as stale (`stream-flush-scheduler.service.ts:52-55`).

Required for those two causes and proportionate: component 1 (`liveSeed` contract half), 3 (projection — the seed's source), 4a/4b/4e/4f/4g (coalescing retention, global sequence, projection feed, seed, deferred anchors), 5, 6, 7 (early claim, superseded-resync guard, seed at fence close, status fallback), 8 (seed-at-close half), 9 (probe fallback). The global sequence (4b) is the minimal fix for cause (2); nothing simpler is equally correct (a per-tab counter kept forever was rejected in D-4 for good reason). 4c/4d repair a third, independently verified loss path (revealed tabs marked visible before their snapshot, `chat-stream-delivery-coordinator.service.ts:143-148` before `:147-149`) — required for the reveal path to be sound at all.

Not required for the two headline causes, and the honest scope decisions for the user:

- **The D-10 cluster — component 4i, component 10, the `resyncTabIds` contract field (component 1's revision-2 half plus `chat-rpc.schema.ts`/spec), the `overflowedDuring` record (component 8's revision-2 half), the D-10 halves of component 7 and metrics, R-B8/R-F7, and two extra MODIFY files.** It exists to repair the fence-bound overflow during a resync's span — a pre-existing hazard whose *consequence* the seed design itself worsens from "chunks lost from the render" to "chunks reversed/duplicated" (the seed applies after the early-delivered chunks). Simpler equally-correct-for-the-two-causes alternative: the plan's own rejected option (a') — abort the overflowed resync and keep today's outcome for that one interleaving, with no fresh-resync request. That deletes the whole cluster (~3 components' worth of surface, one contract field, two spec suites) at the cost of re-exposing the original bug in the narrow window where 2000 live events land inside one resync's fenced span. The plan rejected (a') citing requirement (a) "any length of hidden period"; that is a defensible reading, but it is the single largest scope cut available, and the user should make it explicitly.
- **The D-9 cluster — the fence reservation (component 8) and the wait/recency machinery in component 7.** It repairs the resync↔compaction/plain-load interleaving (round-1 major), not the headline causes. Simpler alternative: today's skip (a resync arriving during a compaction is dropped). That re-exposes the bug whenever an overflow reveal coincides with a mid-turn compaction — plausible on exactly the long, context-heavy turns that trigger compaction. Given the requirement, the wait is proportionate; without it, D-10 also shrinks (no extended wait span to overflow), which is the strongest argument for cutting both together if the user chooses the narrower fix.
- **Component 2 (coalescer)** is only partly forced by the causes: the seed alone restores correctness after an overflow. What forces it in this plan is the constraint that the reproduction spec passes *unchanged* (`ws-switch-resync.spec.ts:43-46` asserts `snapshot.events` containing `delta-255`, which only the coalesced no-overflow path produces) plus its independent value: without it, ~4 s of hidden text overflows and forces a full JSONL reload per hide period. Amending the repro spec to accept the resync+`liveSeed` answer would remove the hard dependency; the reload-frequency reduction would remain as soft value. Keeping it is reasonable; it is the one component whose necessity is partly a spec-letter choice rather than a correctness need.

No component is unnecessary *given* the plan's own requirement reading ("any length of hidden period", spec unchanged); the two clusters above are the honest cost of that reading, and both revert cleanly (optional contract field, loader/replayer-only changes).
